Learning New Robot Skills from Video Demonstrations

We test whether videos of a missing robot skill can be converted into action-conditioned world-model training data. An inverse dynamics model predicts robot actions from video, the resulting trajectories are added to the world-model dataset, and generated rollouts are evaluated with objective metrics, MOS ratings, pairwise human preferences, and qualitative examples.

Problem and idea

Can video-only skills become world-model training data?

Action-conditioned world models need action-labeled examples of the behaviors they must simulate. For new skills, collecting synchronized robot observations and actions is expensive, while videos are easier to generate, retrieve, or record. The paper studies a controlled substitute: remove a target skill from the action-labeled Fractal data, recover actions for videos of that skill with an IDM, and train a world model on the resulting pseudo-labeled trajectories.

Experimental design

Controlled skill-removal experiments on Fractal

DatasetFractal / RT-1 robot manipulation data with RGB observations and 7-dimensional Cartesian action commands.
Held-out skillsKnock and open/close commands are removed from action-labeled world-model training data.
IDM labelingVideos of the missing skills are labeled with an inverse dynamics model following the GR00T-Dreams/DreamGen workflow.
World modelsExperiments use two controllable video world models: CTRL-World and Matrix-Game 2.0.

Main results

IDM labels usually improve over no-action data and approach full-data behavior

MOS trendHuman MOS results show that IDM-labeled data often improves over the no-action baseline. Some confidence intervals overlap, so MOS is treated as descriptive evidence.
Pairwise equivalencePairwise human preferences stay close to 50%, and exact binomial TOST supports practical equivalence within the pre-specified [0.40, 0.60] margin.
Qualitative behaviorRollouts of WMs trained with IDM-labeled data show almost equal quality to full-data WMs.
